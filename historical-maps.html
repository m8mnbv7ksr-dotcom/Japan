Historical Cartography

Historical Maps of Japan

Maps offer a glimpse into how Japan was surveyed, represented, and understood across different periods.

Explore four maps and their collection records. Each map link opens the image or digitized map record from its holding institution. Dates and attributions follow the collection information where available.

01 · Edo period

Japanese Map from 1687

Nihon kaisan chōrikuzu (also known as Nihon ōezu or Dai Nihonkoku no zu)

Made in Japan in 1687, this map depicts the country's regions, roads, and important towns. It also records information about local rulers. The map offers insight into how people in Japan represented their country during the Edo period.

View map image and source — Library of Congress

02 · Surveying Japan

Inō Tadataka’s Map of Japan (1821)

Survey work completed in the early 19th century; the map was presented to the shogunate in 1821.

Surveyor Inō Tadataka and his team traveled around Japan and measured the land and coastline. Their observations were used to produce detailed maps, among the most accurate representations of Japan available at the time.

View digitized map and source — Library of Congress

03 · City map

Map of Edo, circa 1850

Edo (present-day Tokyo), Japan

This woodcut map depicts Edo’s streets, neighborhoods, and notable places near the end of the Edo period. It offers a view of the city’s layout before Edo became Tokyo.

View map image and source — Wikimedia Commons

04 · European cartography

European Map of Japan

Frederik de Wit, circa 1620–1680

European mapmakers drew on travelers’ accounts and other sources to depict Japan. Some details were inaccurate, but these maps show how Japan was understood in Europe during this period.

View map image and source — New York Public Library
