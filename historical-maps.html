Historical Cartography · Japan

Historical Maps
of Japan

Explore how Japan and its cities were mapped across time—from an Edo-period map of the country to detailed plans of Edo, today’s Tokyo.

These digitized maps reveal changing ideas about Japan’s geography, travel routes, regional boundaries, and city life. Select a map image or source link to view the original collection record.

01 · Edo period

Nihon kaisan chōrikuzu

Japanese map from 1687

Made in Japan, this map depicts the country’s regions, roads, and important towns, offering a glimpse of how Japan was understood during the Edo period.

View map and collection record — Library of Congress ↗

02 · Surveying Japan

Inō Tadataka’s Map of Japan

Surveyed in the early 19th century; presented in 1821

Inō Tadataka and his team traveled around Japan to measure the land and coastline. Their survey produced some of the most accurate maps of Japan of its time.

View digitized map — Library of Congress ↗

03 · City plan

Plan of Edo, circa 1752

Edo (present-day Tokyo), Japan

This historic city plan offers a view of Edo’s urban layout during the middle of the Edo period, when the city was the shogunate’s political center.

View image, details, and license — Wikimedia Commons ↗

04 · Woodblock cartography

Woodcut Map of Edo, 1849

Late Edo period · Edo (Tokyo)

This Japanese woodcut map shows the city’s streets and neighborhoods near the end of the Edo period, before Edo was renamed Tokyo.

View image, details, and license — Wikimedia Commons ↗
