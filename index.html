03 — Overview

My connection
to Japan

I chose Japan because it is my country and is situated at the convergence of four major tectonic plates; Japan is one of the most geologically dynamic nations on Earth.

Japan has a long history of isolation and rapid change. As an island nation, Japan developed its own political and social systems.

Japan has one of the largest economies in the world and is known for its advanced technology. Because Japan has limited natural resources. It depends on industries such as car production, manufacturing, and technology.

Japanese culture values harmony, respect, and nature. Traditional arts include tea ceremonies, calligraphy, and wooden buildings.

01 — PLACE

Maps, Location & Cartographers

Maps help us understand Japan’s position in the Pacific and the varied landscapes created by uplift, deposition, and oceanic influence.

02 — FORCES

Plate Tectonics, Earthquakes & Volcanoes

Interactions between tectonic plates contribute to Japan’s earthquakes, volcanic arcs, and mountain building across the archipelago.

03 — CHANGE

Weathering, Mass Wasting & Erosion

Rain, temperature changes, gravity, and flowing water break down rock and shape steep valleys, slopes, and sediment transport pathways.

04 — WATER

Fluvial Processes, Oceans & Coastlines

Rivers transport sediment from highlands to the sea, while waves and coastal currents reshape beaches, deltas, and harbors.

05 — CLIMATE

Climate Controls, Biomes & Climate Change

Latitude, elevation, seasonal winds, and ocean currents influence Japan’s climate zones, ecosystems, and vulnerability to environmental change.
